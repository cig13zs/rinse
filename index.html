Rinse

Photos carry hidden data: where they were taken, on what camera, when. Drop one in to see exactly what it's leaking, then download a clean copy. It never leaves your browser.

Why this is different

Most "remove EXIF online" tools upload your photo to their server to strip it, which means handing a stranger the exact file you were worried about. Rinse does the whole thing in your own browser tab. The image never touches a network.

Nothing is uploaded

No server, no API, no account. Open the Network tab and watch. Rinse never makes a request.

It shows you first

Before you wipe anything, you see the actual GPS coordinates, camera and timestamps hiding in the file.

Total wipe

The clean copy is re-encoded from pixels only. Every EXIF, GPS, thumbnail and maker note is gone.

How the wipe works

Rinse draws your photo onto a blank canvas and re-encodes it. Metadata lives in the file's headers, not in the pixels, so a fresh encode simply has none. That's also the one tradeoff: JPEGs and WebPs are re-compressed once (quality kept high), and the embedded colour profile is dropped along with everything else. For a photo you are about to post, that is fine.
